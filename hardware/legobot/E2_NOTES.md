*D3 (E2_PLAN.md, written before the runs): adopt a tuned finalist only if, on the confirm slice (6,000,000-6,000,099 x 3 tilts, 60 s), (a) its paired per-scenario median-cost ratio to model-based has a 95% bootstrap CI entirely below 1, and (b) McNemar does not show more falls. 'Shows more falls' := the finalist fell in more discordant scenarios than model-based (only_this_fell > only_reference_fell, i.e. more falls in total on the same scenarios) AND the exact two-sided McNemar p < 0.05. Otherwise model-based. If several finalists qualify, the one with the lowest DEV objective is adopted: the confirm tier accepts or rejects, it never chooses among candidates. No multiplicity correction across finalists.*

*Candidates: 'best' = the run's CMA-ES best sample (comparison.json entry 'cma-es': the lowest training objective the CMA-ES search saw), alongside 'cma-es (mean)'; E1_NOTES.md 5.4 ('cma-es (mean) and best-sample finalists'). DE and random search are excluded unless --best overall / --include-entry.*

**Pattern (as in E1):** the tuned PIDs beat model-based on the dev objective *only through fewer falls*. Every tuned
candidate's median per-scenario ratio to model-based is > 1, so model-based has the lowest median cost of all 42
candidates.

**D1 fallback considered and declined (Evan, 2026-11-25 09:27).** The D1 trigger for switching the selection to a
median/trimmed objective was arguably met: the mean objective here mostly tracks fall luck. Evan chose to keep
the model-based PID as the E2 reference rather than re-select under the fallback, because:
- the tuned searches converged consistently (finalists 71–83 on dev, train objectives clustered);
- the falls form a shared hard tail: every controller falls on mostly the same scenarios;
- the discordant falls are coin flips (McNemar p 0.5–1.0 on confirm);
- the mean objective fits fall luck, so re-ranking by a different statistic of the same runs would be choosing
  after seeing the data.

The model-based PID is reproducible from the design formula (`model_based_gains()`) and has the lowest median
cost of all candidates.

**Adversarial audit of E2.2 (reviewer-e22, 2026-11-25): PASS.** It found no frozen-seed touches. Its notes:
- The D3 McNemar rule ("more discordant falls AND two-sided p < 0.05") is effectively a one-sided α = 0.025 test.
  It doesn't matter here: the discordant counts were 6/5, 3/6, 4/5.
- Bootstrap robustness: a 100-seed cluster bootstrap and a tilt-stratified bootstrap both keep every CI lower
  bound ≥ 1.061.
- `--best overall` gives the identical 3 finalists. DE beat CMA-ES on train in only 4/20 runs, and those runs' dev
  scores miss the top 3.
- Model-based wins 86–89% of the confirm scenarios head-to-head against each finalist. The finalists' lower means
  come from 1–3 coin-flip falls.

Tests generalized from the Pololu-only `"PID, ML-tuned"` to `CONFIG.learning.reference_pid_entry`
(test_policy, test_linear_stability, test_compare_controllers). test_legobot_learning now asserts the frozen
model-based reference (not provisional, gains = `model_based_gains()`, B1 sits on it). These modules no longer
skip under `ROBOT=legobot`; LEGO-specific triage is listed below.

- **Harness fix (test_policy).** `_batched` and the residual `pid_batch` built the batch robot with
  `BatchRobot.from_params`, which drops the LEGO slow-PWM/actuator model. That gave ~50% cost gaps against the
  one-robot engine. They now use `robot_from_eval_config(config)`, which is the same path BatchEvaluator uses, so
  training was never affected. The one-robot policy and the direct PID were already exact.
- **Gate b (test_policy).** On Pololu it stays the per-scenario check. On LEGO the L-PID policy and the reference PID
  diverge chaotically after a fall-edge event: the slow PWM quantizes, and tiny float differences flip coin-flip

### Root cause of the CPU-vs-torch train gap (seed 1) and the `tolupsigma` stop

Scripts are in the operator scratch: `diag_s1.py`, `diag_s1_followup.py`, `diag_s1_falls.py`. All scoring is f64 on the
E2 train-150 tier. The GPU checks used the laptop GPU briefly while seed 3 trained; they are read-only and don't affect it.

**The gap is not a backend bias. It is CMA-ES fitting torch's exact chaotic realization.**
- **Exact reproduction.** The torch f64 evaluator reproduces the in-run number bit for bit: 7.789618614925632 is the
  selected checkpoint's `train_objective`. NumPy f64 gives 7.914169.
- **Tick-by-tick on one train scenario.** Seed 1, tilt −0.15, delay 3 steps, run on CUDA vs NumPy:
  - Step 0 is identical, and so are the noise lookup and the latency/PWM tick alignment.
  - The first difference is at step 18: 1.3e-18 in x_dot and 5.6e-17 in motor speed. These are last-bit
    differences, the GPU's sin/cos rounding noted in `test_torch.py`.
  - The difference then grows smoothly, with no jump at any tick: 1e-12 at step 444, 1e-9 at 763, 1e-6 at 1055,
    1e-3 at 1358 (3.4 s).
  - The worst scenario overall (train seed 98, tilt +0.05) shows the same pattern: first difference 4e-16 at step 70,
    1e-6 at step 1013.
  - So it is the pulse-density/backlash chaos the torch tests already document, not a structural difference between the engines.
- **Per-scenario costs are chaotic on either engine.** NumPy against itself with the start tilt nudged by 1e-15: all
  450 scenario costs move by more than 1e-6, 392 of them by more than 1 % (max 20 %). Torch vs NumPy: 374 move by
  more than 1 % (max 24 %).
- **Paired mean, (torch − numpy) / mean ± scenario-level SE:**

| Parameters | torch − numpy |
|---|---|
| final (CMA-selected on torch) | **−1.57 % ± 0.26 %** |
| final, NumPy nudged by 1e-15 (control) | −0.004 % ± 0.28 % |
| final × (1 + 1e-3·N(0,1)), 4 draws (decorrelates the chaos) | −0.05, −0.23, −0.05, +0.63 % (each ±0.27–0.30 %) |
| frozen MB PID (never tuned on either engine), both-up scenarios (n=423) | +0.76 % ± 0.52 %; falls 20 vs 20 |

  Only the parameters that CMA selected on torch show a gap. Unselected parameters show none. So the
  in-run train (and checkpoint-selected dev60) numbers are optimistic by ~1.6 % (dev ~0.5 %). This is a winner's
  curse on the realization the optimizer saw, not numerics. The CPU re-score is the honest number.
- **Fall flips under nudges.** Across 9 unselected realizations, there were 3 falls in 4050 scenario runs (~0.07 %):
  - NumPy with nudges 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 × 1e-15 gave 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0 falls.
  - Torch with nudges 1, 2, 3 × 1e-15 gave 1, 0, 1 falls.
  - Each fall was a different scenario: seed 43 at +0.05, seed 142 at −0.15, seed 39 at −0.15. All fell at
    t = 6.4–6.7 s.
  - The torch-exact realization has 0 falls. Each fall adds ~3.7 to the 150-seed objective. Without falls the mean is
    7.89–7.94 on every realization. So seed 1's expected train objective, falls included, is ~9 (PID 92.9).

**`tolupsigma` is a converged plateau, not a weight blowing up.**
- **Gens 380–539.** CMA's sigma grew from 2.5e3 to 2.0e6 while the actual per-coordinate sampling std (`median_std`)
  shrank from 2.7e-5 to 1.8e-9. The covariance collapsed faster than sigma grew, which is exactly what
  `tolupsigma` (sigma/sigma0 > 1e20·sqrt(max eig C)) detects.
- **Weights are stable.** max |param| over every checkpoint from 4.5e8 to 1.242e9 is 0.9754–0.9868; at gens
  390–539 it is 0.98589–0.98592.
- **No real progress late on.** Best train went only 7.790 → 7.773 and the generation median 7.809 → 7.773. Given
  the result above, that late "progress" is fitting torch's realization at a 1e-7–1e-9 step size.

**Implication for E2.6 (adopted 2026-11-25; see "Notes for E2.5–E2.6 selection"):** a single CPU re-score also
carries realization luck: about a 1-in-3 chance of one fall over 450 scenarios, ±3.7. The selection re-score is
therefore pre-registered as the mean over K = 5 NumPy f64 realizations (nudges k × 1e-15, k = 0…4). Falls per
realization, plus median and both-up costs, are reported. The MB PID is scored the same way.
